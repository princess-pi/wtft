# Chart library spec

A library is only a library once someone has reused it. The chart has one user, wtft. This page is the second: a booth at the state fair, sold by item and by time of day, drawn by wtft's own `buildWtftLines` with nothing changed. Where the chart won't do what the booth needs, that's a finding.

The picker draws the booth twice. The first picture is what the chart returns. The second is the same lines after a table of substitutions, and shows what a caller would get if the chart took its own words from the caller. The second picture is a mockup. The substitutions are the list of places the chart says something only wtft means.

## The booth

One sale is one interaction. The chart reads these fields and nothing else of the interaction.

| Booth | Chart field | Note |
|---|---|---|
| when the sale rang up | `timestamp` | a bin is an hour, a day, or a count of sales |
| revenue | `cost` | dollars, always |
| units sold | `inputTokens` | the chart's second measure is tokens |
| item | `_cat` | one of the chart's 14 categories |
| rush hour | `surgePriced` | an orange row |
| the power went out | `cacheMiss` | a `Cache Miss` line under that row |
| the booth's name | `sessionNameSuffix` | optional |

Six items ride on six of the chart's categories: lattes, apples, funnel cake, pie, corn dogs, lemonade. Souvenirs, when ticked, ride on `other`. The stack order is the chart's, not the booth's.

## What worked without a change

- A stack per bin, coloured by category, as a running total or as this bin's own amount.
- Scatter for the per-bin revenue, and `▃` carryover against `▇` new for units sold.
- Four number columns, and their compaction and dropping as the terminal narrows.
- Bins of minutes, hours, days, weeks, or a count of sales. `-i 25t` is every 25 sales.
- A date line where the day changes, in any timezone, and a scale line with ticks.
- Rows for a rush hour in bold orange. The chart calls it surge. The booth calls it lunch.
- Padding rows out to the limit.

## Findings

Each row is a place the chart is about wtft. **Fix shape** is what a caller would hand the chart. A **caller word** is a string. A **caller list** is a list of categories with a name and a colour each. **Standing** means the chart does something else, and no fix is proposed.

## What the chart can't say

- **Time of day across days.** F16. A bin is a date and an hour. Three days of lunch rush are three separate rows.
- **A bar per item.** The row axis is time, never a category.
- **Anything but two measures.** The columns are one currency and one count. A booth that sells by weight has no third.
- **A caller's own divider.** The rules between rows are the cache miss and the date. The power cut rides on the first.

## Open decisions

These wait on the reviewer and are listed on the tracking issue, so a decision lands in one place.

- **Q-A.** One chart with caller-supplied words and categories, or two charts on a shared bin-and-encode core.
- **Q-B.** Whether the timeline strip belongs to the library or to wtft's title line.
- **Q-C.** Whether the option-to-chart function is extracted first, so both pickers call the CLI's own copy.
- **Q-D.** Whether time of day across days is a second chart, or out of scope.
- **Q-E.** Whether the turn-stride lines the old picker offered, every 10 and every 100 turns, stay gone.
